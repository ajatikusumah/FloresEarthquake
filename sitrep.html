Situation overview

Executive summary

BMKG, Ministry of Health, BNPB, BPBD NTT and IHCP sources

A shallow M7.7 tectonic earthquake occurred offshore approximately 30 km northeast of Mbay, Nagekeo, producing shaking up to MMI VII and a locally observed tsunami at 10 monitoring locations. BMKG reported a peak sea-level observation of 1.61 m in the Reo/Manggarai coastal area and 235 aftershocks by 14:00 WIB, ranging from M2.5 to M6.2. BNPB recorded 47 deaths at the 18:48 WIB cut-off. The Ministry of Health report dated 15 August separately recorded 46 deaths and 113 injured people—36 seriously injured and 77 with minor injuries—without stating a specific cut-off time. The one-death difference is retained rather than reconciled by assumption. BNPB's 17:00 WIB damage snapshot listed 157 houses heavily damaged, 41 moderately damaged and 148 slightly damaged, together with 87 education facilities, 18 health facilities, 5 places of worship and 6 offices affected. The Ministry of Health separately classified structural damage at 9 hospitals and 68 puskesmas; these figures are not combined because definitions and cut-offs may differ. Animal-health impacts and official monetary damage-and-loss estimates were not yet available. All figures remain subject to continuing verification and reconciliation.

Operational implications

Priorities for the first 24 hours

1

Search, rescue and triage

Prioritize Lorens Say/Maumere Port and collapsed-building sites; activate trauma referral and standardized casualty registration.

2

Building and health-service safety

Conduct rapid safety assessments of hospitals, health centres, schools, hotels, places of worship, ports and bridges before reuse.

3

Displacement and vulnerable groups

Map displacement sites and needs for water, sanitation, medicine, pregnant women, children, older people, persons with disabilities, tourists and chronic patients.

4

Risk communication and aftershocks

Use one official message source; prevent return to cracked buildings and beaches, tsunami misinformation and aftershock-related injury.

Consistent public messaging

Remain calm and use official BMKG, BNPB and BPBD channels. The tsunami warning has ended, but do not enter cracked or damaged buildings. Stay alert for aftershocks, protect your head during shaking, and move to an open area once shaking stops. Follow local government instructions before returning to homes, schools, hotels or public facilities.
